Door, Window and Flyscreen Issues: What to Note Before Making an Enquiry

A practical way to record a sticking door, worn window fitting or damaged flyscreen before contacting MEL ONE in Adelaide.

Published 2026-09-18

Describe what happens in normal use

Name the item and its exact location, such as an internal bedroom door, patio screen or window in a particular room. Describe what you observe: catching, looseness, torn mesh or a handle that does not operate normally, without deciding why it happens.

Everyday impact is useful context. Note whether access is awkward, a room cannot be used as expected or the issue appears only when opening in one direction. Do not force the item, take apart hardware or test a lock or window mechanism.

Collect a clear, safe record

One wider photograph can show where the item sits, while a closer image can show the visible concern. Take photos from a stable accessible position and include the affected part without altering it to make damage more visible.

You can add an approximate age if known, an existing product label if easily visible and whether the issue has changed. Photos do not replace assessment; broken glass, security hardware or an affected escape route should be left untouched for appropriate assessment.

Make the next conversation specific

Send the location, observed issue, desired outcome, Adelaide suburb and preferred reply method in one message. If a tenant, owner or property manager is involved, identify who can discuss access and who can approve proposed work.

An initial enquiry begins a scope discussion rather than confirming a method, booking or final cost. Ask what information, assessment or specialist involvement may be needed before a proposed scope is agreed.

Key notes

  • Name the room or outdoor location and the item involved.
  • Describe what happens without assuming the cause.
  • Take wide and close photos only from a safe accessible position.
  • Do not force a door, window, screen or security fitting.
  • Include suburb, access limitations and the best reply method.

Keep in mind

  • This is general enquiry guidance, not repair instructions or a report of customer work.
  • An enquiry does not establish a booking, inspection result or final price.
