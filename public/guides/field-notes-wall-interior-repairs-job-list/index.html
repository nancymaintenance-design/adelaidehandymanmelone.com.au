Wall Marks and Interior Repairs: A Clearer Way to Prepare Your Job List

How Adelaide households can describe small wall, fitting and interior-finishing concerns for a MEL ONE enquiry.

Published 2026-09-18

Separate visible observations from assumptions

State what you can see: a scuff, small surface hole, loose trim, cupboard handle or wall mark, plus the room and approximate size where that is easy to judge. An outcome such as making a surface presentable is more useful than naming a material or process that has not been assessed.

Apparent surface issues can have different causes or may need more information. Do not open a wall, remove covers, scrape unknown material or turn a small job into a do-it-yourself investigation just to prepare an enquiry.

Photograph the condition and surrounding space

A wide image shows the wall or fitting in its room, while a closer picture can show the visible condition. Keep personal documents, family photos and valuables out of frame where possible, and take photos in normal light rather than modifying the surface.

For assemblies or fittings, include a product name, instructions and whether boxes or parts are already present. Do not purchase replacement materials based only on this guide; suitability, quantities and supply arrangements need discussion for the proposed work.

Turn small tasks into an organised request

Group related indoor tasks by room. A wall patch, loose cupboard handle and unfinished shelf can be listed together while retaining one line for each. Flag items that affect daily use, access restrictions, deliveries or another person's approval.

A list helps MEL ONE understand the request, but it does not mean each task is included or can be combined. Scope, materials, timing and any quote are discussed against individual details and site conditions.

Key notes

  • Record the room, visible condition and desired outcome.
  • Add an approximate size only if it can be observed safely.
  • Take context and detail photos without moving fittings or private items.
  • Include product instructions and available parts for assembly tasks.
  • Group tasks by room and identify who can approve the work.

Keep in mind

  • This is general enquiry guidance, not repair instructions or a report of customer work.
  • An enquiry does not establish a booking, inspection result or final price.
