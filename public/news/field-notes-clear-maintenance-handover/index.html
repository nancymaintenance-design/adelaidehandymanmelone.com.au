MEL ONE Field Notes: A Clearer Maintenance Handover

What to include when a homeowner, tenant or property manager passes a maintenance request to MEL ONE.

Published 2026-09-18

Make the location unambiguous

A note saying 'the back door sticks' can mean different things to different people. Identify the room, door or outdoor area, describe what happens and say when you noticed it. Add photographs only where they can be taken safely.

MEL ONE enquiries can start with everyday language. A concise description is more helpful than an assumed diagnosis, especially where a specialist may need to assess the cause.

Clarify who is involved

If a tenant, owner or property manager is involved, identify the person who can approve the work and the person who will arrange access. Do not send access codes or keys in an initial enquiry. Arrange the necessary access details directly after the visit and scope have been confirmed.

List timing preferences or practical limitations, such as furniture in the work area or a shared entrance. These details frame the conversation without implying that a particular time is available.

Keep a record of the agreed next step

Keep the description of the proposed work, exclusions and next action together. If the request changes, raise the change before assuming it is covered. A clear handover is useful whether the next step is more information, a visit or specialist assessment.

Key notes

  • Describe what you observe and the outcome you want.
  • Discuss scope, access, timing and any quote directly with MEL ONE.

Keep in mind

  • This is original general enquiry guidance, not repair instructions or a report of customer work.
  • An enquiry does not establish a booking, inspection result or final price.
