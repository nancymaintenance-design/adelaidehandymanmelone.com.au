# MEL ONE customer-first bilingual copy

Reviewed locally on 2026-10-21. These writing rules do not authorise publication, new services or pricing. Follow AGENTS.md and the translation source-lock process.

1. Lead with the customer's fitting or problem, the work we actually handle, the relevant assessment or practical condition, then a real enquiry action. Write our service descriptions as We / Our / 我们. Do not mechanically replace the legal entity, brand headings, quoted customer speech, image descriptions or third-party attribution.
2. Use the approved business record and recorded job facts. Research keywords and photographs do not authorise new trade capability, supply arrangements, insurance, licences, discounts, prices, warranties or availability. Record missing facts internally; never turn an unknown into either an offered or prohibited service.
3. Explain the actual condition: sound frame, compatible hardware, safe access, manufacturer instructions, supporting material or required permission. Avoid vague doubts about whether we offer a confirmed service. Keep significant safety warnings and specialist boundaries close to the relevant task.
4. State what the particular written quote covers. Separate labour, parts/materials, assessment or call-out charges, travel, waste handling and GST where relevant. Preserve units and all known conditions. Do not invent a universal rate, free assessment, fixed total, all-inclusive scope or automatic multi-job discount.
5. A submitted enquiry, an assessment appointment, an accepted repair quote and completed work are different stages. Buttons and success messages must describe the stage reached. Opening hours do not reserve a visit or promise emergency attendance. Keep contact details and delivery/error guidance consistent in both languages.
6. Describe a real case's problem, work and recorded result. Retain the Modbury fence assessment-only status. Explain the checks needed at another property without repeatedly discussing evidence limits. Preserve factual photographs and captions; never invent measurements, brands, outcomes, locations or customer testimonials.
7. Write natural Australian English and Simplified Chinese with equivalent promises. Keep the same scope, exclusions, costs, permissions, timing and action in both versions. Review text around inline links as a complete sentence. Follow existing flyscreen, quote and service-visit terminology.
8. Maintain service-card titles and accessible names, core H1, layout, map, contacts, footer, language pairing, URLs and technical policies. Do not restore removed citation links in visible text or machine-readable output.
9. After editing source content, extract translations, translate and review the actual affected IDs, record only those reviewed source bindings, build, run bilingual and relevant full tests, and inspect desktop/mobile output. Keep FAQ/schema and existing feeds/llms descriptions consistent with visible content.
10. Candidate-word searches are review aids, never deletion rules. “No”, “may”, “confirm”, brand names and safety terms can be necessary and accurate. Preserve legal/privacy text and report concerns separately. No global pronoun, negative-word or keyword replacement is permitted.

Internal audit and before/after evidence: `../reports/2026-10-21-customer-first-copy/` relative to the site directory, outside the generated public output. Review the scope and new facts before any later production approval.
